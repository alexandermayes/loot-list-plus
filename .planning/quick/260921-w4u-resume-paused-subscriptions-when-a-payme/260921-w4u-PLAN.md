<objective>
Make the "Add payment method to turn it back on" button keep its promise, without first having to discover whether Stripe's billing portal resumes a paused subscription on its own.

Purpose: earlier today Checkout began sending `payment_method_collection: 'if_required'` with `trial_settings.end_behavior.missing_payment_method: 'pause'`, so a trial that ends without a card now lands the subscription in Stripe status `paused`, which `tierForStatus` maps to `free`. Quick task 260921-vns then shipped a paused state in `BillingSection.tsx` whose only action is a button reading "Add payment method" that opens the Stripe billing portal. Whether that portal alone resumes the subscription is genuinely unresolved: Stripe's trials documentation says a subscription paused this way "remains `paused` until explicitly resumed", while its portal documentation elsewhere describes revoking access "until they add a payment method and the subscription resumes". Three documentation fetches did not settle it, and human-check B of 260921-vns was recorded NOT RUN. If the portal does not resume, that button is a lie: the officer adds a card and nothing happens.

Output: a `payment_method.attached` case in the Stripe webhook that resumes any still-paused subscription belonging to the attaching customer, plus a small tested module holding the decision.

The design deliberately does not answer the open question. It is correct in both worlds, because the answer only ever changes whether `stripe.subscriptions.list({ customer, status: 'paused' })` comes back empty. If the portal already resumed, the list is empty and the handler is a no-op. If it did not, the list holds the subscription and we resume it. The `status: 'paused'` filter is simultaneously the correctness argument, the idempotency guard against Stripe's at-least-once redelivery, and the reason the ordinary-checkout case (a brand new customer attaching their first card) costs one API call and nothing else.

As a side effect, this finally answers the question rather than continuing to dodge it: the log line records whether Stripe returned zero paused subscriptions (the portal did it) or one that we then resumed (we did it). Human-check B reads that line back.

Scope boundary: `app/api/billing/checkout/route.ts`, `lib/billing/sync.ts`, `lib/billing/tier.ts`, `lib/billing/subscription-view.ts`, `app/(app)/guild-settings/components/BillingSection.tsx`, `app/api/billing/portal/route.ts` and the database schema are all off-limits. Three files change, two of them new.

Tracer-first decomposition is not applied, and the reason is the same one 260921-vns gave: there is no architecture here to prove end-to-end. The webhook, its signature verification, the tier mapping, the sync, and the `customer.subscription.resumed` case that catches the far end of this change are all shipped and working. What is missing is one branch and the decision inside it. The plan is therefore the decision, the wiring, and the gate.
</objective>

<list_not_first_element_decision>
The constraint asked for a decision on whether a customer can have more than one paused subscription, handled either way with the reasoning stated. **Decision: iterate the whole list, and catch per element rather than around the loop.**

Why not `data[0]`:

1. **The API returns a list and makes no promise of length.** `subscriptions.list` answers `{ data: Subscription[] }`. Reading `[0]` is an assumption about Stripe's data wearing the costume of concise code. There is no cost to being right here.
2. **It is reachable in this product, not just in theory.** `app/api/billing/checkout/route.ts:86` deliberately reuses a guild's existing `stripe_customer_id` rather than minting a new customer, so one Stripe customer is long-lived across that guild's whole subscription history. A guild that trialled, paused, later resubscribed, and paused again leaves two paused subscriptions on one customer. Worse, `guild_subscriptions` upserts on `guild_id` (sync.ts:18-20), so our database only ever remembers the newest one - the older paused subscription exists nowhere but Stripe, which makes it precisely the row a `[0]` would strand with nothing to notice it.
3. **The ownership filter is per element, so it only works if every element is examined.** A list of `[not-ours, ours]` returns the wrong answer under `[0]` in both readings: take the first and we resume a subscription we do not own, filter then take the first and we quietly abandon the second guild.
4. **The cost asymmetry is lopsided.** Handling the list is a `for` loop. Getting it wrong is silent: no error, no log, no Stripe event, an officer whose card landed and whose guild stayed dark, and a support conversation with no evidence in it.

Why the try/catch sits inside the loop rather than around it: the subscriptions are independent Stripe objects, and the failure mode we are guarding against is per-object (a subscription whose resume needs an invoice paid, a price that has since been archived). A catch around the loop would let the first bad subscription abort every good one after it, converting a partial recovery into no recovery. Each iteration catches, records the id as failed, and continues.

On pagination: `limit: 100` is passed explicitly, and the second page is not fetched. This is a considered stop rather than an oversight. A customer carrying more than 100 paused subscriptions is a data incident that wants a human, not a loop; and because the log line reports the returned count, the situation is visible rather than silent the moment it happens.
</list_not_first_element_decision>

<test_strategy_decision>
The constraint asked for a justified choice rather than a default, and specifically warned that mocking the Stripe SDK to assert a call was made is usually a tautology test. **Decision: no `app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts` test file. Instead, extract enough into `lib/billing/resume-paused.ts` that the properties worth asserting become assertable without touching the `stripe` module at all. The constraint's warning is correct, and the extraction is the way out of it rather than a reason to give up on testing.**

Against a webhook route test:

1. **Reaching the new branch means defeating the route's front door.** `POST` calls `getStripe()` (a module-level env-dependent singleton) and then `stripe.webhooks.constructEvent(payload, signature, secret)`, which is an HMAC verification. A test must either mock the whole `stripe` module or manufacture a correctly signed payload. `createServiceRoleClient`, `syncSubscriptionToGuild`, `syncPremiumDiscordRole` and `trackEvent` all sit in the same module graph and would need stubbing too, even though the new branch returns before reaching any of them.
2. **The obvious assertion is a tautology, exactly as the constraint says.** Having mocked `subscriptions.list` to return `[{ id: 'sub_1', metadata: { guild_id: 'g' } }]`, asserting that `subscriptions.resume` was called with `'sub_1'` establishes only that a mock returns what we told it to and that a line we wrote calls a function we wrote. It pins nothing about Stripe and nothing about the product, and it breaks on any honest refactor while the behaviour is unchanged.
3. **The one existing route test is not a precedent that transfers.** `app/api/guild-count/__tests__/route.test.ts` exists and is a good test, but it covers a `GET` with a single dependency and still needed a `// @vitest-environment node` pragma plus a hand-built chainable Supabase double to get there. A signature-verified `POST` webhook is a different order of scaffolding, and grep confirms that nothing in the repo's 78 test files mocks `stripe` today. The first one would set the house pattern for every future webhook test on the basis of the least interesting example.

For the extracted module - and this is the part that makes the decision more than "skip the hard test":

4. **Three of the properties the constraints name are control flow, not data, and they are genuinely fallible.** "A Stripe failure must not 500", "handle the list, not just the first element", and "never resume something that is not ours" are all assertions about what our code does when its dependency misbehaves. Feeding a hand-written fake that *throws on purpose* and then asserting our code kept going and returned a result is not a tautology: the fake's job is to break, and the thing under test is our recovery. That is the distinction the constraint is pointing at, and it is why `resumePausedSubscriptions` takes an injected port instead of importing `getStripe` itself.
5. **Injection is already a house pattern in this exact directory.** `lib/billing/sync.ts` takes a `SupabaseClient` parameter, and `lib/billing/discord-premium.ts` does the same and carries a test. The two zero-import modules, `tier.ts` and `subscription-view.ts`, set the pattern for the pure half. This module holds both halves and matches both precedents.

What the unit tests cannot reach: that Stripe genuinely emits `payment_method.attached` for a portal-attached card, that `resume` on a genuinely paused subscription does what its documentation says, and which mechanism un-pauses a subscription in the real portal. All three are in Task 3's `<human-check>`, per `workflow.human_verify_mode: end-of-phase`.
</test_strategy_decision>
